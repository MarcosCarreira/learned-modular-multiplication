# Learned Modular Multiplication

**A small autoregressive "scratchpad" transformer that learns `(a · b) mod p` as an operation — taking raw `(a, b, p)` and reducing the full-width operands *inside the network*, per the SAIR Modular Arithmetic Challenge's compliance ruling.**

Marcos Costa Santos Carreira · Team MCSC (MAC01-T00008)

---

> **Stage-1 official result (SAIR Modular Arithmetic Challenge, Nov 2026): the only submission in the evaluated field with an *Accepted* compliance review.** Of the 32 teams evaluated (130 participants), every other entry was flagged *Warning*, *Late*, or *Error* — including every team that scored 100%, whose hand-coded fixed-program constructions were flagged rather than accepted. Reviewers' note: *"an autoregressive model writes the whole computation as one unconstrained greedy generation… the host only tokenizes the input and reads the answer from a fixed slice. With randomized weights accuracy drops to zero."*

## Result (compliant)

The submitted model takes **raw `(a, b, p)`** — no `a%p`/`b%p` preprocessing — and reduces the full-width operands in-network, which the organisers ruled is the intended core of the task (`operand_bits` is set 2–4× the prime's bit-length on purpose). Official Stage-1 evaluation (100 problems/tier):

| tier 1 | tier 2 | tier 3 | H>90 | overall accuracy |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1.00 | 0.97 | 0.86 | **2** | 0.297 |

Deterministic, **11 s for 1100 problems** on the official hardware (≈27× under the 300 s budget). Submission: HuggingFace [`MCarreira/recurrent-raw`](https://huggingface.co/MCarreira/recurrent-raw) @ `f9a757ab…`. (At submission time the SAIR Playground read tier 3 at 0.69 on the same weights; the official evaluation draw scored it 0.86 — both below the 90% bar, so `H>90 = 2`. The self-consistency experiments below were measured against the 0.69 Playground baseline.)

The design factors the whole task into one learned primitive, `cell(acc, m, digit) = (acc·R + m·digit) mod p`, applied on a fixed two-phase schedule: **Phase 1** reduces `a mod p` (Horner over `a`'s raw digits, `m=1`), **Phase 2** multiplies over `b`'s raw digits (`m = a_red`). The reduction of the large operand is generated by the network; randomise the weights and it collapses.

**Where the wall is: systematic drift.** Tier 3 doesn't clear the 90% bar, and diagnosing *why* is the scientific contribution. The ceiling is **rollout drift**, not capacity — confirmed three independent ways, all negative: (1) a 2× deeper model *lowered* the frontier and overfit the eval; (2) harder on-policy training (scheduled sampling 0.30→0.55) plateaued at the same place; (3) inference-time self-consistency (majority vote over 7 sampled rollouts, submitted as two of the three allowed models) left tier 3 flat — **0.69 greedy / 0.68 @ temp 0.5 / 0.69 @ temp 0.8**. Raising temperature diversified the rollouts and the vote still didn't move, which distinguishes *systematic* errors (the model reduces the same cases wrong every sample) from stochastic noise. Independently corroborated by the concurrent [neural-horner](https://github.com/Robby955/neural-horner) work.

> **Note on the sections below.** The four- and five-tier "scaling" results that follow are real learnability science but were obtained on a **reduced-operand sub-problem** — we pre-reduced `a%p, b%p`, which was later ruled out of spec (it offloads the core reduction out of the network). They are kept, clearly flagged, because the cross-prime generalisation and scaling findings stand on their own; they are **not** valid competition entries. The compliant model above is the one that counts.

### Learnability science (reduced-operand sub-problem — out of spec as a competition entry)

On primes held out of training entirely, the reduced-operand model generalises (it cannot be a lookup: each tier draws from thousands-to-billions of primes) — tier-3 exact-match **0.82 ± 0.03 across three seeds**, held-out ≈ train. And the same recipe, merely widened (one Horner step per operand digit), carries that generalisation across a **doubling of operand length nearly flat**: held-out tier 3 0.93 → tier 4 0.905, within ~0.02. A memorised table falls off a cliff the instant operands grow; a learned algorithm barely bends. That near-flat scaling is the result — on the sub-problem. (Sub-problem submission, out of spec: [`MCarreira/recurrent-v4`](https://huggingface.co/MCarreira/recurrent-v4).)

**Pushed one tier further, to twenty digits.** Widening the *same* recipe once more (`WIDTH 10 → 20`, twenty Horner steps) reaches tier 5 (p < 2⁶⁴, ≤20-digit operands). On the official benchmark this 20-digit model scores **tier 2 0.98 / tier 3 0.90 / tier 4 0.88 / tier 5 0.80** — the operation still generalising to unseen primes a full doubling of operand length further, declining only ~0.18 across a 16× range of operand magnitude rather than collapsing. Two honest notes: tier 5's 0.80 is below the 90% bar (so this model is `H>90 = 3` and the 10-digit model remains the submission), and its tier 3/4 sit a little under the 10-digit model's because a fixed ~11M-parameter budget spreads thin across five tiers of longer sequences. The claim is *"the same recipe reaches 20 digits with graceful degradation,"* not *"wider is uniformly better"* — but the gentle, unbroken slope is the scaling claim in its strongest form. Model: [`MCarreira/recurrent-v5`](https://huggingface.co/MCarreira/recurrent-v5).

**Is the 20-digit ceiling capacity or drift? A depth test says drift.** We ran the direct experiment — the *same* recipe made deeper (8→14 layers, ~11M→~20M, warm-started, tier-5-focused, annealed) to ask whether more parameters clear tier 5. They do not: on the official benchmark the 2× model scored tier 5 **0.72 — *below* the smaller model's 0.80** — and the gap between its in-distribution dev score and the secret-seed official score *widened* with size (~0.06 → ~0.14). The extra capacity went into overfitting the eval, not generalising further. So the twenty-digit ceiling is **drift/generalisation-limited, not capacity-limited** — corroborating the concurrent [neural-horner](https://github.com/Robby955/neural-horner) work — and the remaining lever is exposure-bias / on-policy training (scheduled sampling, DAgger), not scale. A clean negative, and the sharpest single result about *where* the wall is. Model: `MCarreira/recurrent-v6`.

## The idea in one paragraph

A prime-conditioned feed-forward network memorises per-prime multiplication tables and plateaus (~0.5 on tier 2, floor on tier 3), unmoved by capacity, custom data, or reciprocal features. This isn't a tuning problem: Peng, Narayanan & Papadimitriou (2024) prove a bounded-depth transformer cannot compose functions over large domains, and name multi-digit multiplication as exactly such a composition. The fix is to supply depth through **recurrence** — train a small causal decoder to *write out the arithmetic* as a chain-of-thought, Horner-style, reducing as it goes and never forming the full product:

```
acc ← 0
for each digit b_i of b, most-significant first:
    acc ← (acc·10 + a·b_i) mod p
R ← acc        # the last acc is the answer
```

Each generated token is another effective layer, so the decode loop computes what one forward pass provably cannot. The steps are training targets, not hand-coded logic: randomise the weights and the model emits noise. A richer variant (an explicit reduce step, Abacus digit-position embeddings) clears tier 3, and **widening the very same recipe** — WIDTH 5→10, one Horner step per digit — clears tier 4 too, at linear cost in operand length (exactly the composition depth the depth-limit theorem demands). Because the cost is linear and the per-step operation never changes, the method scales by construction; the open question above tier 4 is accuracy, not the time budget — and pushing to twenty digits answers it with tier 5 0.80, a gentle decline rather than a cliff.

## Reproducing

**Training** is self-contained (PyTorch + NumPy). On Apple Silicon / CUDA / CPU:

```bash
pip install torch numpy
python run_recurrent_raw_auto.py    # the compliant H>90=2 model; _auto.py does chunked, memory-safe runs
```

The runners are argument-free — edit the `CONFIG` block at the top and run. Training writes a best checkpoint, a resume snapshot, and a `*.metrics.csv` you can watch for plateaus.

**Official scoring / submission** (`run_check_submission*.py`, `run_upload_hf.py`) additionally requires the challenge's `modchallenge` harness, distributed with the [SAIR Modular Arithmetic Challenge](https://competition.sair.foundation/) — install it so `modchallenge` is importable, then point `SUBMISSION` / `REPO_ID` at the model dir.

## Compliance

The compliant model takes **raw `(a, b, p)`** and reduces the full-width operands **in-network** — no `a%p`/`b%p` in preprocessing (the organisers ruled that pre-reduction offloads the core of the task, and confirmed the `digit_transformer` reference example that suggested otherwise was an error, not a precedent). The answer is produced by trained parameters (weight perturbation collapses it); the scratchpad steps are training targets, not forward-pass logic; no symbolic-math libraries, no hand-coded long multiplication/division, no Barrett/Montgomery/CRT, no input-indexed lookups; deterministic on CPU (the self-consistency variants seed their sampling for determinism). The reduced-operand `recurrent-v2/v4/v5` line pre-reduced the operands and is **out of spec** — kept only as the learnability-science record.
